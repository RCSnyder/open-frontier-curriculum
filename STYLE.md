# Style

Prefer concrete claims, explicit uncertainty, stable terminology, short headings, source-linked factual claims, and observable learner capabilities. Avoid hype, prestige-by-association, and unsupported universals.

## Audience and sentences

Write plain technical English for a serious reader who can learn exact terms.
Prefer actor, verb, object, condition. Name the person or system responsible for
an action. Passive voice is appropriate when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
Put the main claim first, keep subject and verb close, and separate distinct claims.
The 32-word narrative sentence warning is a review trigger, not a scientific law.

Use the shortest common word that preserves meaning. Define necessary technical
terms once, then keep one term for one concept. Use `must` for requirements,
`should` for recommendations and `may` for permission or possibility. Prefer
`measure` to `perform a measurement of`. Name sources rather than saying experts agree.

## Headings and typography

Use sentence-case headings with concrete topics. Oral-defense questions may remain
questions. Use straight quotes and ASCII dashes in source prose. Use `->` only for
an actual sequence or mapping. Use digits for measurements and ISO dates in metadata.
Reference tables may use compact fragments; do not expand every field into narrative.

## Editorial anti-patterns

Remove unsupported superlatives, promotional adjectives, vague attribution,
synonym cycling, theatrical suspense and summaries that merely repeat a premise.
Do not use prestige as evidence. Technical terms such as robust control remain
appropriate when they name a precise domain concept.

The prose linter enforces high-confidence patterns and reports softer readability
signals. Preserved import snapshots and archives are outside current editorial
ownership. Published pages are not exempt merely because they were generated.

## Evidence discipline

Separate observed fact, model output, inference, interpretation and recommendation.
Date volatile claims. State edition, translation, jurisdiction and population where
they constrain meaning. Correct material errors in place and record the change.

Source quality is claim-relative. A primary statement of a framework does not prove
its efficacy. A textbook may be an excellent introduction without being evidence
for a novel causal claim. A reading list does not establish independent scholarship.

Preserve an encounter with the work, object or practice before substituting summaries.
Do not present generated prose, simulated learners or self-issued review labels as
empirical human evidence. Keep AI permissions and independent assessment explicit.
