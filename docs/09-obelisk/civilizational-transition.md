# Civilizational transition model

This document is a **planning model**, not a prediction of an AGI or ASI date. Open Frontier should survive a wide range of capability trajectories.

## What appears structurally important by 2026

### 1. Cognitive output is becoming abundant

Frontier systems are improving rapidly across mathematics, coding, professional reasoning and scientific tasks. Benchmarks designed to remain difficult are saturating quickly. The educational consequence is not “stop learning.” It is that polished output is becoming weaker evidence of human formation.

### 2. AI is becoming agentic

General-purpose models are increasingly combined with memory, computer interfaces, browsers and other tools so they can pursue multi-step goals with less intervention. Agent reliability still falls on longer or messier tasks, but the direction from **answer  ->  action** matters.

Education therefore needs universal competence in specification, permissions, evaluation, provenance, monitoring, rollback, incident response and institutional responsibility.

### 3. The evaluation gap grows with capability

Generation often improves faster than our ability to know whether a system is reliable in the real world. This makes evaluation itself a first-class intellectual discipline: hidden tests, adversarial evaluation, causal evidence, calibration, independent replication and knowing when personal verification is impossible.

### 4. Science may accelerate while experiments remain decisive

AI is entering scientific discovery, biology and medicine, but models still meet an external world that must be measured. This makes experimental competence, metrology, causal inference, laboratory judgment and physical reality contact more - not less - important.

### 5. Work will be recomposed

The ILO estimates that roughly one in four workers globally is in an occupation with some GenAI exposure, while emphasizing transformation rather than simple replacement as the more likely near-term effect. As systems improve, task boundaries, career ladders and skill half-lives may change repeatedly.

The Obelisk should therefore give a person a **portable intellectual substrate plus learning autonomy plus one or more real crafts**, rather than training only for a current job description.

### 6. Synthetic information competes for finite human attention

When text, video, images, arguments, tutorials and personalized persuasion can be generated at scale, the scarce capacity becomes attention and justified trust. Primary sources, provenance, rhetoric, source ecology, historical consciousness and curation rise in value.

### 7. AI will mediate parts of personal life

AI already mediates schoolwork, health information and work; companionship systems have large user bases and may become more common. The Obelisk should neither panic nor assume substitution is benign. It should teach reliance calibration while deliberately protecting human mentorship, friendship, family, consent, privacy and development.

### 8. AI capability is physical infrastructure

Compute is concentrated in data centers, chips, grids, supply chains and capital-intensive organizations. The IEA projects substantial growth in data-center electricity demand. AI sovereignty and infrastructure policy are therefore not peripheral technology topics: they connect engineering to geopolitics, energy, markets and institutions.

### 9. Physical reality is advancing on a different curve

Robotics and autonomous motion are improving, but real uncontrolled household tasks remain far harder than digital benchmarks. This is a warning against building an entirely screen-mediated conception of competence.

### 10. Institutions are lagging

Responsible-AI evaluation, education policy and governance are adapting slower than capabilities and adoption. A future-ready graduate must be able to **redesign institutions**, not merely follow existing procedures.

### 11. Human meaning cannot rest entirely on economic indispensability

If machines become better at much valuable cognitive labor, education cannot justify human worth by labor scarcity. OECD's recent human-flourishing framework similarly argues for agency, meaning and security beyond a narrow human-capital model.

The Obelisk therefore treats culture, relationships, civic life, care, craft, beauty, philosophy and self-authored purpose as central rather than decorative.

### 12. Matter, ecology and history do not disappear

An age of abundant intelligence still has energy systems, water, land, bodies, disease, cities, logistics, ecological constraints, political conflict and inherited institutions. The curriculum must prevent “intelligence abundance” from becoming metaphysical techno-abstraction.

## Design conclusion

The Obelisk should not optimize for being **AGI-proof**. It should optimize for being **capability-regime invariant** wherever possible.

A module belongs in the durable core when its relevance follows from enduring features of reality or human responsibility, not from the weakness of today's model. Volatile technologies and products belong in fast-moving labs layered on top of that core.
