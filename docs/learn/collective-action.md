# Collective action and the commons

**Why can people who share an interest still fail to act together?**

### Intellectual problem

A shared resource can fail through overuse, exclusion, inadequate maintenance or
capture by those who enforce its rules. These are different mechanisms. Calling
all four a tragedy of the commons obscures the diagnosis. A common-pool resource
is not identical to an open-access regime, and state ownership is not identical
to effective monitoring.

Read the relevant arguments in [Governing the Commons](../library/src-hw-leg-011.md), Elinor Ostrom's
[Governing the Commons](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/governing-the-commons/7AB7AE11BADA84409C34815CC288CD79),
and reconstruct the collective-action problem in [The Logic of Collective Action](../library/src-hw-leg-010.md), Mancur Olson's
[The Logic of Collective Action](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674537514).
These are distinct analytical accounts, not interchangeable endorsements of one
political program. Use [The Economy 2.0](../library/src-hw-core-econ2.md), [CORE Econ's The Economy 2.0](https://books.core-econ.org/the-economy/),
units on strategic interaction and institutions, as an introductory comparative
bridge. Bibliographic links do not settle the validity of any particular case.
CORE's CC BY-NC-ND license differs from the repository's license; this lab links
to the textbook and supplies its own exercises, not adapted textbook text.

### Reconstruction and bounded model

Model a fictional irrigation canal shared by upstream and downstream households.
Specify water availability, withdrawal decisions, maintenance costs, observation
limits and a rule for sharing shortages. Derive when each household benefits from
breaking that rule. Then change one assumption: repeated interaction, monitoring
cost, outside employment, unequal landholding or appeal rights.

Do not silently translate local categories of obligation into a universal payoff
function. State what the model omits and which observations could reveal that
omission. Efficiency, legitimacy and equitable distribution are separate criteria.

### Comparative inquiry

Build two small source packets: one historical/local commons and one contemporary
public service or digital commons. Select cases from different institutional and
geographic settings. Record source author, date, genre, intended audience, translation
and archival limitations. Include evidence from affected users, not only rule-makers.

Trace access rules, decision rights, monitoring, sanctions, repair and appeal.
Compare the mechanisms before comparing outcomes. Explain whether an apparent
failure reflects incentives, enforcement, external coercion, measurement, ecological
change or a mistaken model. Absence from the surviving archive is not evidence
that an institution or practice never existed.

### Action and failure test

Audit a consented low-stakes shared resource: a tool library, club equipment store
or shared room. Observe one maintenance cycle. Propose a reversible rule change
with an appeal route and a cost estimate. No surveillance, collection of sensitive
personal data or unauthorized intervention is permitted.

For assessment, receive a new packet in which apparent improvement follows both
a new rule and an external subsidy. Separate the rival explanations. Then confront
a system that meets its efficiency target while excluding a less powerful group.
Explain why empirical success does not decide the normative dispute.

### AI and defense

Internalize the distinctions among resource, rights, incentives and legitimacy.
Reconstruct the model unaided. Retrieve local law and historical facts with source
checks. AI may propose rival explanations after the initial analysis; it may not
invent interviews or become the authority for a legal interpretation.

Defend an actor/rule map, two rival causal accounts, one discriminating observation,
distributional consequences and a rollback plan. A strong response makes uncertainty
operational. A weak response recites design principles or assumes that one country,
ownership form or institutional tradition defines the universal solution.

Use [examiner calibration](../05-formation/examiner-calibration.md). Independent case review and
unseen comparative rounds are still required before branch saturation is claimed.
