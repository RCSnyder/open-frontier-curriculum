# Human Worlds

Evidence, interaction, strategy, networks, collective action, organizations, power, markets, finance, states, law, politics, inequality, demography, culture, development, world economy, conflict and historical change.

## Why this trunk survives deletion

The learner treats people and institutions as noise around technical systems and cannot reason about incentives, power, coordination or institutional change.

## Modules

| ID | Module | Exit capability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HW01 | Social Evidence, Interpretation & Comparative Method | Separate observation, inference, model, interpretation, and normative claim; build competing causal stories and identify discriminating evidence. |
| HW02 | Interaction, Identity, Norms & Influence | Diagnose interpersonal and small-group behavior without reducing it to personality, manipulation, or stated motives. |
| HW03 | Strategic Interaction, Bargaining & Cooperation | Formalize strategic situations, predict changed incentives/information, and state where the model fails. |
| HW04 | Networks, Diffusion, Brokerage & Segregation | Map relational structure and explain how position changes information, opportunity, diffusion, and collective outcomes. |
| HW05 | Collective Action, Public Goods, Commons & Public Choice | Diagnose coordination failures and compare governance arrangements with explicit assumptions and gaming risks. |
| HW06 | Organizations, Bureaucracy, Professions & Delegation | Trace decision and information chains inside organizations and redesign oversight without destroying useful discretion. |
| HW07 | Power, Authority, Legitimacy & Resistance | Distinguish forms of power and explain why rules are obeyed, contested, evaded, or transformed. |
| HW08 | Markets, Prices, Firms & Property | Explain when decentralized exchange coordinates well, where it fails, and why firms/rules/property structures arise. |
| HW09 | Money, Credit, Finance & Macroeconomic Coordination | Trace balance-sheet and incentive channels from households/firms to financial and macro outcomes; analyze crisis mechanisms. |
| HW10 | States, Law, Constitutions & Public Administration | Trace who may make, interpret, implement, challenge, and enforce rules across multiple institutional forms. |
| HW11 | Regimes, Representation, Parties, Media & Political Competition | Explain how preferences are organized and converted into power under different regime and electoral structures. |
| HW12 | Stratification, Inequality & Social Reproduction | Separate distribution, causal mechanisms, and normative judgments; explain persistence and change in advantage. |
| HW13 | Family, Kinship, Demography, Migration & Cities | Reason from demographic and household structure to labor, welfare, politics, urban systems, and intergenerational change. |
| HW14 | Culture, Religion, Ideology, Nationalism & Collective Meaning | Explain how shared meanings organize identity, legitimacy, coordination, conflict, and institutional persistence. |
| HW15 | Development, Growth, Industrialization & State Capacity | Compare mechanisms of long-run development and explain why similar policies diverge across institutional contexts. |
| HW16 | Trade, Global Production, International Institutions & World Economy | Trace gains, losses, bargaining power, and institutional constraints across borders and production networks. |
| HW17 | Conflict, War, Empire, Diplomacy & International Order | Model conflict and bargaining under coercion, uncertainty, identity, and institutional constraints. |
| HW18 | Historical Change, Path Dependence, Social Movements & Institutional Evolution | Explain persistence and change through temporally ordered mechanisms rather than static snapshots or inevitability stories. |
| HW19 | Integrated Human Worlds Diagnosis & Institutional Design | Produce and defend a global, historically situated diagnosis of a real institution/problem and design a reversible intervention with explicit failure tests. |
