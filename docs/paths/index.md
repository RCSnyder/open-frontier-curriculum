# Find your starting point

Start from something you can attempt, not a claim about your level.
Age can change suitable content, support and responsibility; it does not establish mastery.

## An independent learner

Choose one question and attempt its opening task before consulting a solution.

| Interest | Start | Preparation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reasoning and building | [Mathematical reasoning](../learn/mathematical-reasoning.md) | Basic algebra and careful reading |
| Institutions and society | [Collective action](../learn/collective-action.md) | Strategic interaction and evidence; prerequisite routes are on the page |
| Language and interpretation | [Reading literature](../learn/reading-literature.md) | Sustained reading with glossary or accessible-media support |

Use the recorded prerequisites to identify a specific gap. A missing prerequisite
record does not mean a subject is suitable for a beginner. Complete the practice,
try the assessment independently, then ask a knowledgeable person to challenge it.
Keep the work, their objection and your revision together.

## Childhood: encounter with trusted support

These are sample encounters for an adult to adapt, not a complete school curriculum.
They require attention to language, numeracy, disability, safeguarding and local requirements.

- **Capability:** build a paper bridge, predict which shape carries more coins,
  and compare the prediction with observation. Use safe materials and adult supervision.
- **Context:** describe how a shared classroom space is used. Listen to two people's
  explanations before proposing a rule. Do not collect private information.
- **Judgment:** distinguish what you saw from what you guessed. Ask what observation
  would make you change your answer.
- **Representation:** tell a story, draw its sequence, then notice what the drawing
  expresses differently from the telling.

The adult observes explanation, curiosity, revision and care. A child's confidence
or speed is not a ranking of intellectual worth. Formal subject routes follow
when literacy, numeracy and readiness support them.

## Adolescence: increasing independence

Move from following a procedure to explaining and challenging it. Use a mentor
to choose suitable works, identify prerequisites and bound the risks of projects.

1. Complete one [proof sequence](../learn/mathematical-reasoning.md), with support where algebra or language requires it.
2. Undertake a [whole-work encounter](../learn/reading-literature.md) with a suitable edition and content guidance.
3. Study [social evidence](../learn/hw01.md) before making confident claims about institutions.
4. Attempt a simulated [shared-resource inquiry](../praxis/index.md) and defend a revision.

This is one suggested route, not a compulsory sequence or a graduation standard.
Progress depends on demonstrated readiness. No child is required to disclose
personal experiences, conduct unsupervised fieldwork or use an AI service.

## Specialization and renewal

For technical depth, choose one of the [seven specialization routes](../03-specializations/README.md).
Their schedules are planning examples, not guaranteed times to competence.
Use external professional or scholarly standards and qualified feedback.

For an existing craft or field, choose a recent failure or uncertainty. Revisit
the relevant foundation, rebuild an explanation, and test it on an unfamiliar case.
[Learning to learn](../foundations/learning.md) supports this cycle.

## Independent work and AI-assisted work

Make a first attempt without generated answers. Then use AI, where appropriate,
to propose objections or alternatives. Check its sources and reasoning yourself.
Keep the original attempt distinct from the assisted revision. Neither polished
output nor a passing automated check establishes independent understanding.
