# Mathematical reasoning

Five ranked references. **Rank 1 is the default spine, not a requirement to read all five.**

## 1 | How to Prove It: A Structured Approach
**Daniel J. Velleman** | Primary spine | Undergraduate

The cleanest transition from calculation to proof: logic, quantifiers, sets, functions, relations, induction, proof construction.

[Amazon search](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=How+to+Prove+It%3A+A+Structured+Approach+Daniel+J.+Velleman) | No legal full text located

## 2 | Book of Proof
**Richard Hammack** | Open primary / practice | Undergraduate

Excellent proof apprenticeship with abundant exercises; unusually strong legal free option.

[Amazon search](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Book+of+Proof+Richard+Hammack) | [Full open text](https://richardhammack.github.io/BookOfProof/)

## 3 | Mathematical Proofs: A Transition to Advanced Mathematics
**Gary Chartrand, Albert D. Polimeni, Ping Zhang** | Depth | Undergraduate

Broad proof toolkit with a conventional course structure; useful for systematic drill.

[Amazon search](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Mathematical+Proofs%3A+A+Transition+to+Advanced+Mathematics+Gary+Chartrand%2C+Albert+D.+Polimeni%2C+Ping+Zhang) | No legal full text located

## 4 | How to Read and Do Proofs
**Daniel Solow** | Technique | Undergraduate

Compact and methodical; good for learning proof moves and debugging failed arguments.

[Amazon search](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=How+to+Read+and+Do+Proofs+Daniel+Solow) | No legal full text located

## 5 | Proofs: A Long-Form Mathematics Textbook
**Jay Cummings** | Alternate voice | Undergraduate

Conversational but rigorous; strong for independent learners who need motivation plus many examples.

[Amazon search](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Proofs%3A+A+Long-Form+Mathematics+Textbook+Jay+Cummings) | No legal full text located
