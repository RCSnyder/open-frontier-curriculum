# Design judgment studio

Canonical target: **DR08**; interfaces with **DR03**, **DR04**, **DR06**, **DR11**,
**DR12**, **EX-M04**, **ML02** and **TS-F22**.

## Task and reference

Design a low-stakes reservation or equipment-loan interaction for actual users.
Start with paper and observation. Identify the user's goal, permissions, possible
errors, recovery route and accessibility needs before choosing a visual style.

Read Jakob Nielsen's
[10 usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/),
source `SRC-DR-NIELSEN`. They are professional rules of thumb, not universal laws
or a substitute for user research. Reconstruct the distinction between a system
state, a user's mental model and the visible sign that connects them.

## Compare and articulate

Collect three authorized examples of the same task: one clear, one difficult, and
one visually impressive but misleading. Remove brand cues where practical. Rank
them against declared criteria before discussing reputation. Explain tradeoffs in
information hierarchy, affordance, error prevention, feedback, reversibility,
typography and operational cost. Preference is evidence about a person, not by
itself a justification of the design.

Draw the state transitions, including cancellation, invalid input, unavailable
resources, partial completion and loss of connectivity. Compare a diagram with a
paragraph describing the same system. State which mistakes become visible in each
representation and where either representation hides timing or responsibility.

## Make, observe and revise

Build a paper prototype and observe consented participants attempting two realistic
tasks. Do not coach them toward the expected answer. Separate observed behavior
from your interpretation, and record where your own language misled them.
Use synthetic identities and no real financial or health transactions.

Revise one consequential failure. Show the rejected alternative and why it was
rejected. Then test a changed context: limited vision, different language, low
bandwidth, intermittent service or a user unfamiliar with the institution. Do not
simulate a disability and claim to have validated accessibility for that population.

## Compression test

Attempt the task using only technical correctness and rhetorical explanation.
Record whether spatial hierarchy, affordance, embodied use or perceptual comparison
still causes recurrent failures. This is an internal DR deletion/compression probe.
It does not prove that DR deserves permanent trunk status; an independent matched
comparison is needed to distinguish a missing capability from poor instruction.

## AI and assessment

Before using generation, internalize task states and high-cost failure conditions.
Reconstruct the interaction without the prototype. AI may generate alternatives,
but the learner owns selection, accessibility, permissions and evaluation.

Receive an unseen change request after the first review. Revise the interaction,
explain what the change invalidated and demonstrate recovery from a seeded error.
Defend the final choice through the sequence: perceive, compare, contextualize,
articulate, curate, make, revise, defend. A beautiful screen that conceals failure
or removes recourse does not pass. Nor does a correct but unusable interaction.

Use [examiner calibration](examiner-calibration.md). Preserve the observation log,
versions and disagreement. Expert preference and small user samples are evidence
with limits, not numeric guarantees of quality.
