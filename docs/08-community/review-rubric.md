# Curriculum review rubric

A maintainer/reviewer should score substantive proposals against these questions.

## 1. Cross-domain value

Does the change unlock multiple downstream capabilities, or only add local detail?

## 2. Necessity

Can the target capability be regenerated without it?

If yes, the proposal may be valuable but is probably **deferred**, not minimum.

## 3. Transfer

Does the proposed concept reappear across domains?

## 4. Evidence quality

Prefer, in order:

1. primary/official source;
2. authoritative synthesis;
3. institutional teaching material;
4. secondary explainer;
5. AI synthesis.

AI synthesis can organize; it cannot serve as final authority.

## 5. Proof of ownership

Can the learner:

- regenerate;
- solve an unseen case;
- construct a counterexample/failure;
- state assumptions;
- measure/check;
- defend?

## 6. Opportunity cost

What is deleted or compressed?

A curriculum that only grows is not a theoretical minimum.

## 7. System closure

Does the change account for measurement, scale, reliability, economics, safety, or institutions where relevant?

## 8. Epistemic labeling

Normative and practitioner sources are welcome when useful, but must not be presented as empirical consensus.

## 9. Safety

Do not turn the educational repository into operational guidance for harmful biological manipulation, weapons, malicious software, or evasion.

## 10. Adversarial review

The author should state the strongest case against their own change.
