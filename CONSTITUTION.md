# Open Frontier Curriculum

## Purpose

Develop the foundations for independent entry into unfamiliar questions,
specialist study and responsible action. Intellectual independence includes
knowing when to seek a source, a collaborator or qualified professional help.

Minimum means generative foundations: ideas, methods and practices from which
further understanding can be developed. Breadth without reconstruction, criticism
and transfer is insufficient. No fixed collection establishes universal expertise.

## Four complementary foundations

| Foundation | Responsibility | Demonstration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open Frontier: capability | Explain mechanisms; calculate, measure, build and debug | Construct and test a model; expose its limits and failure modes |
| Human Worlds: context | Interpret people, histories, institutions, incentives and power | Reconstruct actors' meanings; compare causal and interpretive accounts against evidence |
| Philosophy: judgment | Examine evidence, reasons, assumptions, values and legitimacy | Reconstruct rival arguments; defend a bounded judgment under objection |
| Expressive Command: representation | Interpret and articulate meaning through language and form | Defend an interpretation; create and revise a representation answerable to its purpose |

These foundations overlap. Their names identify intellectual responsibilities,
not disjoint departments or a proof of an optimal taxonomy. Mathematics belongs
in capability while informing judgment; interpretation connects human worlds,
philosophy and expression. Relations carry these connections without duplicating identity.

## Integration and development

Praxis joins the foundations in investigation, making, deliberation, action and
revision. Assess each responsibility as well as the coherence of the whole.
Technical effectiveness does not erase ethical failure; eloquence does not repair
unsupported evidence.

Development determines suitable encounters, support and responsibility. Childhood,
adolescence, independent study and renewal are routes into the same intellectual
world. They require different pedagogy. The current examples do not constitute a
validated school curriculum or a universal age schedule.

Life contexts connect knowledge to care, work, institutions and stewardship.
Learning practices support acquisition and retention across all foundations.
Mind and learning remains a substantive field as well as a source of such practices.
Visual, spatial and material design remains visible within representation, with
connections to technical practice. No legacy branch has been deleted or academically merged.

## AI and human responsibility

AI is a tool, a subject of inquiry and a change in the conditions of intellectual
agency. It does not define the purpose of education. Keep independent attempts
distinct from assisted revisions. Check sources, reasoning, uncertainty and the
consequences of action. Competence includes recognizing when delegation is inappropriate.

## Editorial obligations

- Distinguish subjects, outcomes, resources, learning activities, assessments and pathways.
- Distinguish required preparation from recommended sequence and related inquiry.
- Explain why a source is relevant and what to read; a bibliography alone is insufficient teaching.
- Give each subject one learner home. Preserve stable identities and compatibility routes.
- Put editorial evidence and taxonomy debates behind the learning experience, while keeping them accessible.
- Label incomplete guidance. Do not manufacture order, scholarly review or learning outcomes to fill a template.
- Test proposed boundaries against material that resists them and against independent learner use.
